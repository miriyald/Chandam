# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Three overlapping audiences, all engaging with Telugu/Sanskrit/Kannada poetry:

- **Scholars & teachers** — University professors, researchers studying prosody. They analyze poems methodically, compare meters across rule sets, and need precision and comprehensive reference material. They use Chandam as a verification and teaching aid.
- **Students & learners** — Telugu literature students encountering meters for the first time. They need clear examples, guided exploration, and a forgiving learning path through 379+ documented meters.
- **Poetry enthusiasts** — People who write or read Telugu poetry recreationally. They want quick meter identification, discovery of new forms, and the ability to test their own compositions.

Context: Users open Chandam to either (a) paste/type a poem and identify its meter, (b) browse and learn about specific meters with examples, or (c) create custom meter rules for personal collections.

## Product Purpose

Chandam (ఛందం) is a Telugu poetry meter analysis system that identifies metrical patterns in verse — the Indian-language equivalent of identifying iambic pentameter. It preserves and makes accessible a 1000+ year literary tradition through modern technology.

Success: a user pastes Telugu poetry and instantly understands its metrical structure, including gana patterns, yati (caesura) positions, and prasa (rhyme) rules.

## Brand Personality

**Modern, clear, confident.**

Like a well-designed reference tool that applies contemporary design clarity to traditional knowledge. Not a museum piece — a living instrument. Authoritative without being intimidating. The interface should communicate "this tool knows what it's doing" through precision and restraint, not ornamentation.

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS / startup UI** — No gradient CTAs, rounded-card grids, stock illustrations, Inter font, purple accents. This is not "every AI tool."
- **Overly playful / gamified** — No bright reward colors, badges, streaks, confetti, cartoon illustrations. Too casual for a scholarly tool.
- **Dense academic / government site** — No Times New Roman, gray backgrounds, walls of text, zero visual hierarchy. Functional but lifeless is not acceptable.
- **Dark mode hacker aesthetic** — No terminal green, monospace-everywhere, code-editor vibes. Wrong cultural context for Telugu poetry.

## Design Principles

1. **Telugu first** — Typography, layout, and spacing decisions are driven by Telugu script requirements. Latin text is secondary. The tool speaks the user's language visually.
2. **Content is the interface** — Poetry text and analysis results are the product. Every design decision serves readability and comprehension of Telugu verse.
3. **Confident restraint** — Strong hierarchy, deliberate spacing, minimal decoration. Let the content breathe. Complexity is in the domain, not the chrome.
4. **Cultural grounding without nostalgia** — Draw from the literary tradition (manuscript aesthetics, temple palette) without being retro or ornamental. Modern tool, traditional knowledge.
5. **Progressive disclosure** — 379 rules is overwhelming. Surface the right information at the right level. Summaries first, details on demand.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.1 AA baseline (contrast, focus, semantics, keyboard)
- Mobile-first: many users access on phones. Touch targets, readable Telugu on small screens is critical.
- Standard font sizes — trust browser zoom behavior rather than forcing large text. Don't assume users need accommodation; ensure the defaults work well and zoom scales cleanly.
- Telugu Unicode support is non-negotiable across all layers.
- Bilingual UI (Telugu primary, English secondary) — layout must accommodate both scripts gracefully.
